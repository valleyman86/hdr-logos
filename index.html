HDR can be visually louder.

A controlled test of how color metadata can make an otherwise identical image stand out like a lantern on an HDR-capable display.

These images have identical decoded RGB pixel values. The HDR version differs by having a Rec.2020/PQ ICC color profile. On a compatible HDR/EDR display and browser, its white areas may appear physically brighter than ordinary SDR white. A screenshot may not preserve the visible luminance difference.

Open SDR image directly Open HDR image directly

If both images look identical, the browser, display, or operating system may not be presenting the PQ image in extended dynamic range.

Why this exists

An HDR image encountered in otherwise ordinary webpage content can command attention in the way a louder advertisement cuts through surrounding audio. This demonstration explores that “HDR loudness” effect—not as a recommendation, but as a reproducible example of why HDR/EDR web imagery could be unusually attention-grabbing.

Pixel identity proof

The comparison is made before color management. A verifier removes PNG scanline filters, compares every stored RGBA sample, compares the compressed image-data bytes, and then compares every PNG chunk other than the profile.
